# Vord appearance

Vord follows the system appearance with a neutral black-and-white palette. Backgrounds, text, selections and buttons use equal red, green and blue channels, with no blue-gray or ivory tint.

The window sits behind a borderless content plane. Review and dictation prompts sit directly on that plane, without an extra card background, outline or shadow. Typography, centered placement and spacing give words their hierarchy. Secondary forms may use a softly separated, borderless surface; input wells and navigation selections retain distinct neutral values.

Primary text stays brighter than metadata. Small tertiary text remains legible on content, raised cards, input surfaces and selected navigation. Primary buttons use a soft white fill in dark mode. Fine rules belong to inputs and information separators; the large content rectangle and study prompts have no outlines. Increased-contrast appearances raise metadata and control-border contrast. State is also expressed with labels and marks, not color alone.

References:

- [Apple: Dark Mode](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/dark-mode) — adaptive appearances, sufficient foreground contrast and background depth.
- [NN/G: Dark Mode issues](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dark-mode-users-issues/) — dark shadows can lose their depth cues; surface separation and text readability need deliberate treatment.

Keep reading areas matte and calm. Decorative gradients, luminous outlines and saturated accent colors are not part of this palette.

Interface typography uses the rounded system design for navigation, headings, controls, metadata, statistics and explanatory text. The AppKit capture field resolves the same rounded system font descriptor. Dedicated vocabulary headwords keep their serif reading style; Chinese study prompts keep their existing system fallback. Default labels inherit the shared interface body font.
